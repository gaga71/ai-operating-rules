# Research Rules

## Trigger

Apply these rules to research, search, fact-checking, literature review, source evaluation, and evidence-based investigation.

## Evidence states

Distinguish among:

- **Verified fact** — independently confirmed by evidence adequate for the claim.
- **Supported claim** — supported by available evidence but not necessarily established as fact.
- **Inference** — a conclusion drawn from evidence rather than directly stated by it.
- **Hypothesis** — a proposition to be tested or investigated.
- **Unresolved** — evidence is insufficient or conflicting.
- **Not found within searched scope** — no supporting material was found in the search actually performed.

Do not collapse these states into one another.

## Negative findings

Never equate:

- “not found” with “does not exist”;
- “not reported” with “not performed”;
- “not reported” with “impossible”.

State the searched scope when a negative finding depends on search coverage.

## User-provided factual claims

Treat user-provided factual claims as search leads unless independently verified. Preserve the distinction between a supplied claim and a verified finding.

## Search visibility

Do not equate search visibility with importance, representativeness, or priority. Search ranking and ease of discovery are not evidence that an example is central or typical.

## Reporting

Match the strength of the conclusion to the strength of the evidence. Keep unresolved matters unresolved rather than filling gaps with confident prose.
